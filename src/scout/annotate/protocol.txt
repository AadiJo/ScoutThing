AI ANNOTATION PROTOCOL: marking one robot's position in an FRC match video

You are making ground truth: where one robot is, about once a second, in one match video.
A tracking tool will later be scored against your marks, so your marks must be correct,
evenly spaced and made without looking at anything the tool produced. A wrong mark is
worse than no mark. You work only through four commands (run from the repo root):

  uv run --no-sync scout mark-protocol                      prints this text
  uv run --no-sync scout mark-frames RUN AT... [options]    gridded images of the video
  uv run --no-sync scout mark-check RUN MARKS.csv           your marks drawn back as rings
  (the coordinator runs scout mark-compile on your file afterwards)

RUN is the run folder you were given (e.g. runs/Qualification_16). Open the PNG paths the
commands print with your image reading tool.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each frame of the broadcast has a scoreboard at the top, a wide MAIN camera view, and two
small corner camera views at the bottom that show the two ends of the field closer up.
Only the main view counts: every mark you write is a pixel in the main view. The corner
views (--view corner_left / corner_right) are only for reading bumper numbers.

Each robot is a square base about 0.95 m wide wrapped in a BUMPER RING: a padded band
about 13 cm tall, all the way round the base, in alliance colour (red or blue) with the
team number printed in white on it. Six robots play: three red, three blue.

Coordinates are SOURCE-FRAME pixels: x from the left edge, y from the top edge of the full
1920x1080 frame. The images are scaled and cropped, so the only way to get coordinates is
to read the labelled grid lines. NEVER estimate a coordinate from where something sits in
the picture you are viewing (your view of the image is scaled; its pixels are not source
pixels). Find the two labelled lines on each side of the point and interpolate.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHICH POINT TO MARK
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mark the CENTRE OF THE BUMPER RING: the middle of the robot's square base, at bumper
height. Not the top of the robot, not the middle of the robot's visible body, not the
nearest bumper edge, not the bumper numbers.

The main camera looks down on the field from high up, so the ring appears as a squashed
four-sided shape: the near (front) bumper low in the picture, the far bumper higher up and
usually hidden behind the robot's body. To find its centre:
  - x: the middle of the ring left to right. Use the outside ends of the near bumper, or of
    both side bumpers where visible.
  - y: halfway between the near bumper band and the far bumper band. If the far band is
    hidden, follow a side bumper back from the near corner to where it disappears; the
    centre is halfway along the side bumper. The far band is roughly as far above the near
    band as the robot's width looks foreshortened, which is much less than its width.
  - Ignore everything above the bumpers (arms, turrets, hoppers, shooters). A tall robot's
    body rises far above its ring centre; marking its visual middle puts the mark too high,
    which casts onto the field too far from the camera.
  - Mark the middle of the band's height (the band, not the carpet below it).
Typical sizes on an official broadcast main view: robot 80 to 200 px wide; the ring is
about 30 px deep (near band to far band) in the far half of the field and 60 to 90 px deep
close to the camera, so the centre is 15 to 45 px ABOVE the middle of the near bumper band.
Do not mark on the near bumper band itself. A mark 15 px off is a large error.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. IDENTITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The robot is identified by its bumper number and nothing else.
  - First get the roster: `mark-frames RUN <t> --view scoreboard` shows the six team
    numbers (left group one alliance, right group the other; their colour tells which).
    Only these six numbers are possible.
  - At the start of your segment, read the number on a bumper. Use a tight zoom in the main
    view (--size 160x120) or a corner view when the robot is near an end of the field.
  - Re-confirm the number every time you lose the robot: after it passes behind a hub,
    a referee, a field element or another robot, or after any second you could not see it.
  - Robots of one alliance share a bumper colour and often look alike; some numbers look
    alike at low resolution (6, 8, 9, 0; 1, 7). Shape and colour of the robot's body help
    you FOLLOW a robot between reads; they never replace a read. Write down in your notes
    what the robot looks like once you have confirmed it (e.g. "low black box, open top").
  - Following without a read: continuity counts only if you saw the robot in every assigned
    second since the last read and it never touched, crossed or overlapped a lookalike. If
    it crossed a same-alliance robot, you need a new read before marking it visible again.
  - A mark whose identity rests on a guess gets state `unsure` (it is left out of the
    truth). Never write `visible` on a guess.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STATES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  visible    You can see the robot's bumper ring (or enough of it to place the centre) and
             its identity is confirmed (section 3).
  estimated  The robot is hidden (behind a hub, a person, another robot) but you know where
             it is: it went in and has not come out, and you can place the ring centre to
             within about half a robot. Mark where the centre is, not the edge of what hides
             it. Say in the note what hides it.
  unsure     You can place a robot, but you are not sure it is this one.
  skip       You cannot say where the robot is, or it is outside the main view (robots do
             drive out of the picture at the left and right edges and along the near
             wall). Leave x and y empty and say why in the note.
Partly hidden but the centre can still be placed (half the ring visible): `visible`.
Seen through the clear near wall: `visible` if you can place the ring.

BEFORE you write `estimated` or `skip`, look at the WHOLE main view (--size all) for that
moment and, near an end of the field, the corner view. "It went behind X" is only true if
it is nowhere else. In the trial a robot "hidden behind the camera operator" had in fact
driven along the near wall and out of the left edge of the picture; seven marks were
3 to 4.5 m wrong because only half the view had been searched.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHICH MOMENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mark exactly the times you are assigned (usually every whole second, e.g. 70, 71, 72 ...).
Do not move a mark to a nearby moment where the robot is easier to see, and do not add
extra marks at interesting moments. Every assigned time gets one line: a mark, or `skip`
with a reason. Hard moments left out would make the tracker look better than it is.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. INDEPENDENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do not open, read or list anything the tool produced in the run folder: tracks, labels,
identity, detections, reads, facts, calibration, grade, bench, overlay video, or any
*_check.jpg. Do not look at other truth files. Use only the commands above and what you
see in their images. If you saw tool output by accident, say so in your report.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE MARK FILE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CSV, one line per assigned time, first line exactly the header:

  team,at,x,y,state,note
  2839,f4195,1043,512,visible,read 2839 on near bumper (zoom)
  2839,f4255,1060,505,visible,followed; same black box
  2839,f4315,,,skip,behind red hub since 71.5 and two red robots near it
  2839,f4375,1090,470,estimated,behind red hub; entered at 72.3 left side

`at` is the frame printed on the image as f<number> (preferred) or video seconds. Notes may
contain commas. Lines starting with # are comments. Write lines as you go (append to the
file after every batch) so nothing depends on an image you can no longer see.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE WORKING LOOP
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Images cost a lot of context (each about 1,500 to 2,500 tokens). The loop that worked:

  a) Start. `mark-frames RUN <t0> --view scoreboard` for the roster, then
     `mark-frames RUN <t0>` (whole main view) to find the robot, then a tight zoom
     (--around X,Y --size 400x260 --zoom 2) to read its number.
  b) Guess, then check. Write the next 3 to 6 assigned times into a separate batch file
     with a guessed position (the last known one) and note "p". Run
       mark-check RUN batch.csv --out <dir>
     Each cell shows one line as a magenta ring on its own frame: a 3x zoom with a grid on
     top, and a 480x240 crop at 1x below. Use the lower crop to find the robot and read its
     number, the zoom to read the ring centre. Six cells per sheet.
  c) Correct each line from the sheet, append the corrected lines to your mark file.
  d) Check the corrected lines with mark-check again when any moved by more than about
     15 px, or when you placed them from a wide image. Fix what is off.
  e) Lost robot (not in the lower crop of its cell): one wide image of several moments,
       mark-frames RUN f6173 f6233 f6293 --around 960,450 --size 1400x430 --per-image 3
     then go back to b). Before writing estimated or skip, see section 4.
  Keep the batch to six or fewer lines so the sheet fits one image; delete old images.

Read coordinates only from the grid labels of the image you are looking at: find the two
labelled lines either side of the point and interpolate. Your view of a large image is
scaled down, so "about 900 px across the picture" is not x=900.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. WHAT TO REPORT AT THE END
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Counts of lines per state (visible, estimated, unsure, skip) per robot.
  - Every moment identity was established or re-established, and from what (which view,
    which digits you could read).
  - Every stretch you did not mark as visible, and why.
  - Anything you were unsure about, and anything from section 6 you saw by accident.
  - Images opened and the mark file path.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. LESSONS FROM THE TRIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From a blind trial against dense human truth (two robots, 80 assigned times):
  - Typical error of a careful mark is 15 to 25 px (0.2 to 0.4 m). Most of it is the far
    band being hidden: the depth of the ring has to be judged. Near the camera a robot is
    150 to 200 px wide and the ring 60 to 90 px deep, so judge x from the ring's outer
    corners, not from where the number is printed.
  - Easy mistake 1: reading a position off a wide image by eye instead of from its grid
    labels. It put three marks 75 to 105 px off. The check sheet caught all of them.
  - Easy mistake 2: following the wrong robot after it leaves the crop. A same-alliance
    robot drove into the crop where the target had been; only its bumper number showed
    the swap. Re-read the number whenever the robot left your crop.
  - Easy mistake 3: calling a robot hidden without searching the whole view (section 4).
  - Robots with similar bodies on one alliance were separated only by bumper numbers.
    Read them on the lower crop of the check sheet; at 1x a facing number 10 px tall is
    readable on the official broadcast.
  - Easy mistake 4: copying a position from the wrong cell. Sheets hold several moments;
    the worst error in the trials (200 px) was the next second's position written on this
    second's line. Read the time in the cell caption before writing each line.
  - Easy mistake 5: misjudging x on a big robot close to the camera (150 to 200 px wide)
    from the 1x crop. Errors of 90 to 130 px happened there. When the robot fills most of
    the zoom tile, check x against both outer corners of the near bumper.
  - Corner views differ by broadcast. On some they are low cameras aimed across the field
    or at the crowd and show nothing useful; look once, then stop using them if so.
  - Cost: about 0.45 images per mark with the guess-then-check loop (about 1,200 tokens
    per mark in all), about 0.8 with a separate locate image for every moment.

Harder footage (low camera close to the field, strong lens distortion, offseason event):
  - Robots are 40 to 60 px wide in the main view and bumpers are often hidden behind the
    near wall, a referee or the field edge. Numbers were readable in about 1 moment in 4.
  - Several robots can share one body type (e.g. black open-top boxes). Expect most marks
    there to be `unsure` unless a number is read every few seconds. Do not raise them to
    `visible` because the body looks right.
  - Bumper colour may not match the alliance at an offseason event. Use the number only.
  - Straight field lines bend near the image edges; ignore them when judging the ring
    shape and use the robot's own bumpers.
